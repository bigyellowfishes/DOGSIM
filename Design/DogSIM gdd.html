2. First Minutes & How to Play

3. Core Loop

# Step (verb) What the player does (Player input → what they see or hear → what changes) Why do it again?
1 Meet picked The centre starts with one pup at a time; upgrades let it hold more, up to 5 at once (the creator's current thinking, not final). Upgrades come from the centre's star rating picked. The arrivals kennel door slides open and a pup runs in. You click the pup → an action menu opens above its head and its hearts bar appears → the pup is selected. Every new pup is a new set of needs to read. picked
2 Care picked Each pup arrives with a few visible needs that come from its traits: one is muddy, one is hungry, one is shy picked. You read the pup and pick the action it needs from its menu (such as Wash, Eat or Play), in any order → the pup does it, with hearts and speech bubbles → each need you meet adds a heart, and the pup gains confidence. Confidence is the process a pup goes through to get ready to be adopted. The hearts bar (5 hearts, called happiness in the build) shows it draft. Each need you meet adds a heart you can see and brings the pup closer to a home. picked
3 Lead picked You press Follow → the pup trots after you through every room and up to the park → you press Unfollow to stop. A need can send you to another room: the bath for a muddy pup, the park for a restless one picked. What the pup needs is in another room. picked
4 Match picked A pup has the confidence to be adopted → you make the match from profiles of potential owners, and a pup can go to any home → the pup leaves through the adoption kennel and the Adopted counter goes up. If the home is not right, the pup may come back to you with less confidence, and you go through the process again. A good match makes room for the next pup; a pup that comes back needs you again. picked
One complete loop takes A loop ends when you match a ready pup with one of the potential owners and it leaves through the adoption kennel. A match that is not right can reopen the loop: the pup comes back. Target: a player can complete one loop in 5 minutes. That is longer than 90 seconds, so the earlier payoff is every heart; the first lands at about 15 seconds. to be decided: time it once needs and matching are playable
Decision, challenge, or expression Which home each pup goes to. Pups have traits and potential owners have profiles. Any pup can go to any home, so the choice is yours, and a match that is not right may come back. You can also keep one dog as your own pet: it always stays with you, and if the right owner comes up you can choose to let it be adopted. You keep a pup by picking your own card at the Match step picked.
Shortest satisfying visit / typical session 5 minutes, one full loop / open-ended: a session can last as long as the player wants.
Why repetition 10 differs from repetition 1 Each pup arrives with different needs, because its traits differ, and the potential owners' profiles change too. So you have to read every pup and every match again. picked to test H2-01

Pillars.

  1. Every pup gets a happy ending, in the end: setbacks yes, losses never. A pup can come back as many times as it takes; it is never taken away and there is no game over. picked
  2. Overly cosy, pastel and cute.
  3. The match is yours: any pup can go to any home, and the game never chooses for you. picked

4.3 Two return hooks

Selected hook Exact trigger or timing What the player anticipates Reminder channel + no-reminder fallback
1. Collection: the doggidex An entry is stamped when a match sticks. A pup that comes back is not stamped until its next home works out. picked Completing a big book of twenty or more breeds. picked
2. Your own dog: the daily wish picked One new wish each day (a run in the park, a bath, a nap by the window), changing at a fixed hour open: waiting on picking the hour; the audience is in Brazil. Granting it adds a day to "days together". A missed day pauses the count and never resets it. Tomorrow's wish, and a growing "days together" count on the dog's collar tag, which other players can see in the Park. Fallback with no reminder: the dog's bed shows tomorrow's wish before you leave.

The creator names a third reason to come back: to help other dogs. That is the core loop itself.

How you get your own dog: at the Match step your own card sits among the potential owners. Pick yourself and the pup stays. You can have one dog at a time, and the option appears from your second pup, about five to eight minutes into the first visit. Later your dog can appear in a match too, which is how you let it go to the right home. picked

5. Social by Design
The repeatable social loop Player A brings a dog to the Park and taps Player B's dog to offer a play-date → Player B taps to accept → the two dogs play and both gain hearts, and a shy pup gains confidence it cannot get alone → the friendship is saved: the dogs greet each other next time, and a daily wish can be "see my friend again", which is the reason to regroup. picked to test H2-03
The disappearance test Play-dates and dog friendships stop: "see my friend again" wishes cannot be granted and shy pups lose their fastest way to gain confidence. The centre, the matches, the doggidex and the rest of your dog's wishes carry on. draft
From strangers to a group Everyone in the Park is a carer with a dog, so there is one role. A dog that wants to play shows a bubble, and you tap it. No voice or shared language is needed. draft
Recognition & continuity Every player is a chibi carer they can dress, with a few choices such as hair and jumper colour picked. A name shows over each carer in the Park, next to the centre's stars draft. Dog friendships persist: friend dogs greet each other the next time they meet, and a daily wish can ask to see a friend again.
Quiet hours & player counts Players meet in the Park: every player who goes there can see all the other players there. When few people are online, a solo player can play everything, and a resident Park dog offers play-dates so no pup ever waits for another player. Social play becomes viable at 2 players; the ideal group is 4 to 6; the v1 tested maximum is 10 players. The daily wish often sends dogs to the Park, so players who are online at the same time pass through the same small lawn. picked
Drop-in / drop-out A player who arrives late can offer a play-date at once. If a partner leaves halfway, your dog keeps the hearts it earned and the resident Park dog finishes the play-date. Saying no costs nothing. Each dog can receive one offer at a time, and no other player can touch anything inside your centre. picked
Visible play (the bystander test) You see carers walking their dogs across a small lawn, and two dogs chasing each other while their carers watch. draft
Shareable play (the memorable moment) The graduation: a pup you cared for trots out under the bunting to its new family, and its stamp lands in your doggidex. picked
Bring-a-friend When your dog's wish is "see my friend again", you want that one player in the Park. Once they arrive, the two dogs run to greet each other and the wish is granted for both of you. draft

6. Mobile-First

Every core-loop verb on touch.

The whole game is point and click: one click per action. On touch, each click is one tap draft to test H2-04. Built and tested on desktop only so far.

Core-loop verb How it works with touch controls
Meet Tap the floor to walk. Tap the pup to open its menu.
Care Tap the action the pup needs.
Lead Tap Follow, then tap where to walk. Tap the glowing exit to change rooms, and step on the arrow tile to reach the Park.
Match Tap one of the owner profile cards, or your own card to keep the pup.

UI plan.

Small screen first: the camera has four on-screen buttons in a corner (rotate left, rotate right, zoom in, zoom out), so phones keep the orbit picked. The pup's actions open as large buttons within thumb reach draft. Built so far on desktop: Arrived and Adopted counters, a hearts bar while a pup is selected, and the action menu above the pup. The dev menu is for testing only.

Performance.

Target: 60fps on recommended desktop hardware and 30fps on a named phone in the mobile client, both at 10 players, our v1 tested maximum. to be decided: the test phone is not named yet The biggest risk is the Park at its maximum: 10 carers and 10 dogs, all animated at once. Plan: a player's centre shows only their own pups, and the Park shows one dog per player plus the resident dog. draft

Desktop-only dependencies.

Two, both in the camera: zoom on the W/S keys and orbit by dragging. Decentraland's mobile documentation (checked 2026-11-24) lists the mobile client as touch only, with no keyboard shortcuts and no drag or swipe gestures. The on-screen camera buttons replace both, so nothing in the loop needs them. The scene also runs its own cameras, hides the player's avatar and works out taps with its own ray picking; the documentation does not say whether these work on mobile. to be decided: check the current build on a phone with the Creator Hub QR code Names over carers will be our own text labels, because the platform's name tag component is listed as missing on mobile.

7. World, Look & Story

Story / world.

You run a snug little day centre for dogs waiting for a forever home. You look after each pup until it is happy and ready, then wave it off to its new family.

Visual direction.

A little isometric diorama: four rooms in a ring around the building's centre, one visible at a time, with a park above. Simple and clean, in flat single colours with no busy textures, and a pastel palette of mint, coral, lavender, pink and butter yellow. You play a chibi day-centre carer (not a vet): hair in buns with pink bows, a butter-yellow jumper and a mint apron with a pink heart. The two kennels show where pups come and go: arrivals on the left wall (mint with a coral roof, a "Welcome" mat) and adoptions on the right wall (lavender with a mint roof, graduation bunting, a "Goodbye" mat).

Our signature shot is the two doors: the whole cut-away room in one frame, with the mint Welcome kennel on the left, the lavender Goodbye kennel on the right, and one pup between them with the carer. picked

8. Audience & Comparables

Primary player + arrival context.

For players in Brazil, mainly women (men can play too), who already enjoy cosy pet and care games on their phone, arriving alone from Decentraland's Discover page, looking for a five-minute visit with a dog that knows them. draft

How the first group arrives.

The first players come from Decentraland's own Discover page: we list the World well and let players find it picked. We do not count on players overlapping: everything is playable alone, the resident Park dog covers play-dates, and two players are enough for social play to start. draft

Deliberately not for.

Players who want competition, combat or a score to beat: nothing here can be won or lost. draft

Comparables

Comparable A, outside Decentraland: To The Rescue! Comparable B, outside Decentraland: Nintendogs
What we observed works Adopters come in wanting specific things, and you match dogs to them. Every dog has its own traits. The look is pastel and friendly. draft Care is direct: you pet, wash and play with the dog yourself and it reacts at once. Dogs never grow old or die, so it stays gentle. Short visits are enough. draft
What does not fit our audience or context, and why It turns into busy shelter management: money, disease, staff and many dogs at once. Reviewers called it hectic and stressful, and a dog can be sent away. That fights "cosy" and "losses never". draft The dogs are yours forever. Nothing ends, so there is no goodbye and nothing to match. It is a private, solo game built for its own hardware. draft
What we will do differently No money, no admin and no loss: one to five pups in a small diorama. A wrong match is not a failed sale; the pup comes back to you. draft The pups are not yours. You get each one ready and let it go, and you may keep just one. draft

What keeps the experience changing after launch.

  • Without building a new level, we can change or rotate which breeds arrive and the pool of daily wishes every week. draft
  • If an update is skipped, random needs, random owner profiles and the daily wish still create variation. draft
  • N/A: nothing resets, and progression gives no advantage over another player. draft
  • One player behaviour that would change what we build next: if players keep a dog but never visit the Park, we bring play-dates to the centre's front door. draft

Not building in v1.

Nothing is held back: the creator plans the whole design for v1, including all twenty or more breeds, carers you can dress and saved dog friendships.

Top risk + fallback.

Top risk: the twenty breeds. Nineteen new breeds at about 4 hours each is about 76 of the roughly 180 hours planned across the four build weeks, which makes them the largest single item, and the 4 hours is an untested estimate. to be decided: time one breed in week 1 to replace the estimate Fallback: if a breed takes longer, v1 launches with fewer breeds and the rest arrive a few at a time after launch, shown as silhouettes in the doggidex until then. draft
